How to read this planner — click to expand (worth 2 minutes before a board meeting)

What "passing" means. The board set one test for 2027: pay every bill on time — including the final construction payment of roughly $130,000 and the bank loan — and still finish December 2027 with at least $20,000 in the bank as a cushion. The big banner at the top right tells you whether the plan you've dialed in passes that test, and by how much.

Why assessment money is shown separately. The $430 renovation assessment is promised to the bank. The moment a member pays it, it belongs to the loan — it cannot buy chemicals, pay guards, or cover the contractor. This planner enforces that rule automatically: if assessments don't fully cover the $97,183 yearly loan payment, the difference is taken out of operating money; if they collect more than the loan payment, the extra sits locked in a reserve and never shows up as spendable cash.

What a "guard-hour" is. One lifeguard working one hour. If the pool is open 9 hours with 2 guards on the clock (1 actively on stand, 1 backup rotating in hourly), that day uses 18 guard-hours. It's how we convert "operating hours" into payroll dollars, and how we check whether 4 guards can physically cover the schedule.

The April 1 test. The final construction bill comes due around April 1, 2027 — before the pool opens and before most members historically pay. Because enrollment now opens September 1, 2026, the planner shows how many members must be fully paid up by April 1 to cover that bill. For scale: in 2025, only 42 members had paid by April 1. We are also negotiating to move the bill to April 30, which makes this much easier.

Where the starting numbers come from. Every default is pulled from WGRA's own books — the 2024 and 2025 P&Ls, the May 2026 balance sheet, the loan terms from the bank, and the July 2026 board packet. Numbers that are estimates rather than facts say so in the fine print under each slider.
